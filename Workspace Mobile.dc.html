Team Stores · Mobile web · Phase 0 foundations + Phase 1 first pass

Workspace Mobile

Phase 1 scope, as decided: model 1b; source of truth is the desktop Workspace file only — no flow or feature exists here that doesn't exist there. First pass: Store Home, Settings, workspace level; second pass (D): My Teams, Products, Orders, Reports. Full drill-ins, edits as full-screen pushes, confirmations as sheets. Store chip on Home only; switching stores from depth means back to Home first. Only the Customizer is “desktop only”; the store wizard is built. Components are condensed versions of desktop's (HomeAttentionCard, DetailCard, WsPill, ChoiceCard, SavedAssetCard…), named in each caption.

Navigation models, chrome, page patterns and the spec they rest on. iPhone 16 frame, 393 × 852pt. Built as a web page in Safari — the Safari toolbar and the safe areas are drawn where they'll really be. No feature screens; the demonstration screens below exist only to test the patterns against real strings.

What had to be re-solved

Tables · Depth · Density

Tables → “Row + drill-in”

One pattern for every table: each table row becomes a list row showing the identifier, one key metric right-aligned in tabular figures, and one meta line. Tapping the row opens the record with every remaining column as a label / value list (the tertiary template). Sort and group-by move into a sheet opened from a chip row under the title. Totals pin as a footer card.

Commission report → rows = reps, metric = commission total, meta = gross · orders · stores
Sales breakdown → rows = whatever the group-by is (team / product / code), metric = revenue, meta = orders · AOV
Roster matrix → rows = players, metric = “4 / 6 items”, meta = the missing items by name. A By player | By item segmented control flips the axis, because the matrix's column read (“who's missing the jacket?”) is a real question
What it costs: no cross-column scanning — a rep can't eyeball three metrics at once the way the desktop table allows. Rejected: horizontal scroll with a frozen first column (readable, but every row action is off-screen and the SE shows 1.5 columns), and stacked label/value cards in the list (the 148-player roster becomes 148 × 180pt of scrolling).

Depth → collapse, then Jump

Desktop's worst chain is six levels. Two of them are removed on mobile, not navigated:

desktopworkspace → store → My Teams → division → team → roster → player
mobileStores → store Home → My Teams → team → player
  • Division becomes a section header inside the My Teams list (teams grouped under it), not a screen. Division-level actions move to the header's overflow. Cost: a league with 30 teams across 6 divisions is one long list — the search icon and a division filter chip cover it
  • Roster is the team screen. There is nothing on the team level that isn't the roster plus a header. Cost: the ordering-window per team moves into the team header card
  • What remains is four levels under the hub, every one with a named back, and the Jump to sheet from level three down. No breadcrumbs as tap targets, no persistent “up” button
Density → the right rail is demoted, not stacked

Desktop Home: main column = status strip, attention cards, store sections; right rail = managers panel, storefront URL, recently worked on. On a phone the question is what a rep opens the store for: to check and act, not to look things up.

  1. Store chip + one-line status — where am I, is it open, how long left
  2. Attention cards, red → blue → grey — the reason to open the app; max 5 then “Show all”
  3. Section index — the six tabs with counts; the red-dot rule carries over
  4. Recently worked on — promoted from the rail because on a phone resuming beats browsing
  5. Point of Contact · storefront URL — one card, last. Reference data: tap to open or copy
Dropped from Home entirely: the per-manager list beyond the Point of Contact (lives in Settings → Managers), the theme colour swatch, and the structure counts for leagues (they're on the My Teams row).

  • 44 × 44pt minimum for anything tappable; icon buttons are exactly 44 with a 22–24pt glyph
  • Buttons 48pt; list rows 56pt (one line + meta), 64pt (with avatar / thumbnail), 72pt (three lines)
  • Chips are 36pt visually with a 44pt hit area (padding on the hit layer, not the chip)

  • Pressed state replaces hover everywhere: background → #f1f1f3 on rows, 85% opacity on filled buttons, 120ms out
Spacing & shape
4 · 8 · 12 · 16 · 24 · 32
  • Screen gutter 16; card padding 16 (desktop 18); gap between cards 10, between sections 16
  • Card radius 12 (desktop 10), sheet radius 20, buttons 10, pills 999. Borders stay #ececf0 1px, no shadows on cards
  • Severity rails keep desktop's 3px left border: #e1251b · #2563eb · #d4d4d8. Never amber on a card
  • Page background #fbfbfc, cards white — identical to desktop
Breakpoints
  • ≤ 430pt — these patterns. Gutters 16. (iPhone SE 375 → same layout; list rows wrap to three lines sooner, the 1c strip shows 3.5 tabs)
  • 431 – 743pt — large phones landscape, small tablets portrait: same patterns, gutters 24, content max-width 600 centred. Sheets become centred dialogs at 480 wide
  • ≥ 744pt — hand off to the desktop layout. Whether that handoff is a breakpoint in one app or a redirect between two is open question 1
  • Device frames in this file are 393 × 852; nothing in the patterns depends on the height
Safe areas & viewport
  • <meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, viewport-fit=cover"> — no maximum-scale; we don't disable pinch zoom
  • App bar: padding-top: env(safe-area-inset-top). Bottom chrome: padding-bottom: env(safe-area-inset-bottom). Toasts offset by the same inset plus chrome height
  • Root is height: 100dvh with an inner scroll container, so Safari's bar show/hide resizes the content, never the chrome
  • Landscape: safe-area left/right insets become gutters; the tab bar (if any) keeps its height
Before feature work

Decisions, conflicts, open questions

iOS convention vs browser reality
Swipe back — Safari owns the edge. Chose: visible named back on every non-primary screen; no custom swipe
Collapsing large title — scroll-linked, jitters under Safari's own collapsing bar. Chose: large title in content, bar title appears on scroll-out
Bottom tab bar — competes with Safari's toolbar. Chose: offered in 1a / 1c with honest heights; 1b avoids it entirely
Pull-to-refresh — Safari reloads the tab. Chose: no; refresh on focus + explicit control
Stacked card sheets — need the parent as an owned layer. Chose: flat bottom sheets with scrim
Native pickers — date/select are Safari's own, styled by Apple. Chose: use them for dates and simple selects (they're accessible and free); sheets for anything with rows that carry meta
Haptics — none. Chose: pressed states and a 120ms colour change carry the feedback
Decisions taken in this phase
  • Typography inherited from the desktop workspace (Archivo + Inter), not from the PROLOOK design-system kit's Gotham — the brief says same family; the two sources disagree and desktop won. Flagging it
  • Division and roster levels collapse; four levels under the hub is the ceiling
  • One table pattern (row + drill-in) with an axis flip for the roster matrix
  • Right-rail content is demoted to the end of Home, Point of Contact and URL as one card
  • Reports and Settings are second-class in every model: a More tab (1a), bottom rows of the index (1b), off-edge in the strip (1c)
  • Store switcher is a sheet in all three models; picking a store resets the stack
  • Errors are inline cards; toasts never carry failures
  • The 11pt tab label is the single exception to the 12pt floor
Decided: 1b. Hub and spoke, no persistent nav; workspace level is Team Stores with the Overview · Stores · Earnings segmented control; store level is the section index with the store chip as switcher and up-target. 1c out (double nav), 1a out (tab bar vs Safari, second chrome). Settled: the chip appears on Home only; secondaries and deeper carry no chip row — back labels name the path, and switching stores means back to Home first. Edits push full-screen; confirmations are sheets.
Open questions
  1. Responsive breakpoint or separate experience? It decides whether 1c (desktop-faithful) is the pragmatic choice and whether the ≥744 handoff is a layout change or a redirect. The patterns above work either way, but 1a / 1b assume they can own the whole viewport
  2. Full workspace or a subset? If mobile is check-and-act (submit orders, assign managers, answer a parent), then Products configuration, the Customizer and most of Settings are desktop-only, and the section index should say so (“Edit on desktop”) rather than pretend. That also makes 1b's cost — Orders ↔ Products hopping — mostly disappear
  3. Does the buyer storefront share this system? Assumed no. Parents get the storefront's own look; this file is reps only. If yes, the chrome inventory needs a second, lighter variant and the store chip must never appear there
  4. Global search? Desktop has section-wide search in the Team Stores bar. Phase 0 scopes search to the current screen; a cross-store search would need a home in the app bar on primary screens
  5. Overview and Earnings on mobile? Both are chart-heavy. If they come across, they need the Reports table pattern plus a chart rule this phase doesn't define
